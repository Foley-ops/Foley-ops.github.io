I'm a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at The University of Texas at San
Antonio, where I work in the [Vision and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory
(VAIL)](https://github.com/UTSA-VAIL) with
[Dr. Amanda Fernandez](https://sites.google.com/view/amandafernandez). My
research is in geometric deep learning: neural networks built on quaternion and
other hypercomplex (Clifford) algebras, models that operate on spherical
lattices, and making those models efficient enough to run on edge hardware.

I'm most interested in *why* a structured model works. Hypercomplex networks
come with an appealing story (built-in rotations, fewer parameters), and a
lot of my work is testing that story with careful ablations: take the model
apart, find which piece carries the signal, and report what holds up and what
doesn't, including when a simpler baseline wins. In
[QSV](/publications/#foley2026qsv), for example, the quaternion's feature
transport turned out to matter far more than its attention routing.

## Background

Before computer science, I served five years in the **U.S. Marine Corps**
maintaining C-130J aircraft, three of them in leadership roles.

My first research was in systems, in UTSA's Cloud and Systems Lab with
[Dr. Palden Lama](https://www.cs.utsa.edu/~plama/), partitioning and
pipelining deep-network inference across Raspberry Pi clusters and edge
servers. That work became [ParetoPipe](https://github.com/cloudsyslab/ParetoPipe)
and a paper at IEEE EdgeCom 2025, and it's why I still measure efficiency on
real devices rather than by parameter count. I finished my B.S. in Computer
Science at UTSA in 2025 as a School of Data Science Undergraduate Research
Fellow and started the Ph.D. in 2026.
